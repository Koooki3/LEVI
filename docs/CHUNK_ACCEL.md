# In-chunk acceleration (experimental)

The workstation's client command has one more switch: **In-chunk acceleration**. With it, the command runs the FR3 collector through an isolated entry point (`lab/chunk_accel/run_collect_accel.py` for rules v1 and v2, `lab/chunk_accel/run_collect_phase.py` for rule v3); the policy server, the collector's gates, clamps, jump checks, keys and recording are unchanged. Rules v1 and v2 work with **plain inference at the policy's 10 Hz only**; asking for them with RTC or VLASH is refused (`chunk_accel_requires_plain`). Rule v3 ([Rule v3](#rule-v3-the-phase-clock)) works with plain, RTC and VLASH. Every rule needs the policy's 10 Hz (`chunk_accel_requires_10hz`) and a site that binarizes the gripper (`chunk_accel_requires_binarized_gripper`). Asking for v1 or v2 without naming a mode means plain inference; asking for v3 without one means RTC, the page's default mode. While the switch is on, a choice under it picks the rule: **v3** (the page opens on it), **v1** (below) or **v2** ([Rule v2](#rule-v2)). Runs are filed under `<model>_accel_demo` (v1), `<model>_accel_v2_demo` (v2) or, for v3, `<model>_accel3_demo`, `<model>_rtc_accel3_demo` or `<model>_vlash_accel3_demo` by inference mode, unless a group is given.

## What it does (rules v1 and v2)

Every chunk the server returns goes through one rule before the collector executes it. A chunk is hurried only if all of these hold:

- no gripper change anywhere in the chunk, including the actions after the executed part (so the approach to a grasp or a release is never hurried), and the same gripper value as the command the arm was last given (the measured state's, which also catches a close the client threw away or sent late);
- at least 1 s of executed actions since the last gripper change (the gripper is still closing or opening). Only actions the collector accepted count: a plan it threw away because of a jump does not shorten the hold;
- the executed actions stay within 5 mm of the straight segment from the first to the last one (a press-and-retract along it is not straight), and the end effector's orientation changes by at most 0.15 rad from the first one.

A hurried chunk is executed as a short ramp from its first to its last executed action, in `m` ticks instead of `n`:

    m = clip(ceil(max(D / (v dt), angle / (w dt), (0.2 s / dt) ln(D / 5 mm))), 2, n)

`D` is the chord, `v` 0.25 m/s, `w` 0.8 rad/s. The last term is sized so that an arm closing in on a held target with a 0.2 s time constant would be within 5 mm of the last action at the next observation. Today's arm does not get that close: in the replay below it was 8.5 mm away (median, 11.2 mm p90) after a hurried chunk, no further than after an unhurried one (8.9 mm median); only the 0.1 s filter reaches about 5 mm (p90 4.9 mm). Along the ramp the target leads by `tau`, the controller's setpoint low-pass (0.2 s, `--accel-tau`), times the ramp speed (the inverse of that low-pass) but never leaves the segment between the first and the last action, and two consecutive targets are at most 5 cm apart (the collector refuses a target 10 cm from the measured pose). Because the hurried chunk is shorter, the collector executes it and queries the policy sooner. After a ramp, at most 3 leading actions of the next chunk that lie behind the ramp's end along its direction are dropped, but only if that chunk keeps moving the same way, so a policy that does not see velocity does not pull the target back.

Each demo records the settings in `policy.chunk_accel` (the session's eval log header too) and the episode's counts in `eval.chunk_accel`, aborted episodes included (LEVI's unattended mode writes them to `eval.abort_detail.chunk_accel`): chunks, hurried, ticks saved, dropped actions, refused jumps, and why the others were not hurried. A chunk with a NaN or infinite pose is passed on exactly as the policy gave it (`not_finite`). The ramp client (rules v1 and v2) refuses to start with RTC, VLASH, a control rate other than 10 Hz, step-by-step mode or an unbinarized gripper. Install it (below) before the first run: the page does not check that the client file exists.

## Expected gain

From the latest evaluations of `pi05_fr3_all_step49999` (10 Hz, read-only analysis in the workspace): 48 % of the executed chunks of plain runs, 54 % of RTC runs and 34 % of VLASH runs are straight and gripper-free; 16–17 % contain a gripper change. With this rule (the gripper hold and the settle term included) 46 % of plain chunks are hurried, and an open-loop replay of those episodes through the identified controller and arm model gives a **median 17.0 % shorter plain episode** (duration ratio 0.830; 0.840 for the total duration of the plain episodes) and 0.859 over all 10 Hz groups, RTC and VLASH paths included; these are the numbers of the study's 2026-11-01 correction (see [Rule v2](#rule-v2) for the source), and before it the median plain episode saved 3.40 s. The farthest any target was from the arm, 70.7 mm, is the unhurried replay's own maximum, on an RTC chunk executed as given (the client refuses 10 cm). In the earlier replay of 2026-10-31 the arm's path stayed within 1.6–2.0 mm (p95) of the unhurried one, and the arm was as close to its target at the next observation as without acceleration (median 7–10 mm). The replay does not re-run the policy, so how the policy reacts, and the task outcome, need the real robot. With the setpoint filter at 0.1 s (`setpoint_filter_alpha` 0.01, approved and compiled 2026-10-31, default still 0.005) pass `--accel-tau=0.1` (the page and a hosted run always use 0.2: add the flag to the copied command): it shortens the lead only; the settle term keeps 0.2 s, because the lightly damped arm still rings (with 0.1 s there too it was 24 mm from the target at the next observation, p90, and peaked at 0.46–0.50 m/s).

## Rule v2

v2 is the second rule of the same switch, for comparing the two on the robot. The page shows the choice (three radio buttons, each with a one-line explanation) only while the switch is on; turning the switch off or choosing another rate keeps the choice but sends nothing, and choosing RTC or VLASH sends rule v3 instead (v1 and v2 run with plain inference only) and brings the choice back with plain. With v2 the command gets one more flag, `--accel-rule=v2`; a v1 command has none and is exactly the command the switch made before. The form field is `chunk_accel_rule` (`v1`, `v2` or `v3`, absent means `v1`); v2 or v3 without the switch is refused (`chunk_accel_rule_needs_switch`), any other value too: the hosted start answers `chunk_accel_rule_invalid`, the preview route a plain 422 that names the field (its request body is typed). The preview names the rule (`control.chunk_accel.rule`) and adds the warning `chunk_accel_rule_v2`; the hosted run record keeps `chunk_accel_rule` (`v1` for a record made before the choice existed), and each demo records it in `policy.chunk_accel.rule`.

**v2 needs this version of the client.** A client installed before the choice existed has no `--accel-rule`: with `--accel-rule=v2` it stops at start on the unrecognized option (the arm does not move), and a v1 command runs on whatever rule that install has, so neither side of an A/B would be this code. The page does not check the installed version. To update, delete `lab/chunk_accel/accel.py` and `lab/chunk_accel/run_collect_accel.py` in the openpi checkout and run `install.py --apply` again ([the adapter instructions](../integrations/chunk_accel/README.md)); the installer refuses to overwrite files that differ.

What v2 adds to v1 (`integrations/chunk_accel/accel.py`; it is the rule "V4g" of the offline study `fr3_accel_test/variable_speed/rule_v2`, in the workspace, outside this repository):

- **Curved chunks.** A chunk that fails the 5 mm straightness test is re-timed along its own path, as long as the end effector's orientation changes by at most 0.15 rad (v1's rotation limit). How sharply the path itself turns is not limited; the arm only slows down at the bends. The line through its executed actions keeps its shape and gets a speed profile limited by 0.25 m/s, by how much the controller's setpoint filter would cut a corner (at most 2 mm, which allows 0.1 m/s² sideways with today's 0.2 s filter), by 0.8 rad/s and by 1.5 m/s² along the path, ending at rest on the last action. The target leads along the path, never off it. Straight chunks keep v1's ramp.
- **The actions before a gripper change.** The executed actions before the first gripper change (at least 2, at least 1 s after the previous change, passing the same tests) are hurried the same way. The hurried part ends on the action before the gripper change and holds it 2 more ticks (0.2 s), so the gripper command goes out with the arm settled; from the gripper action on, the policy's own pace. A change only after the executed part makes the whole executed part such a prefix.
- **A 6 cm guard.** A hurried plan gets more ticks until no target is predicted more than 6 cm from the arm (a 0.2 s first-order approach from the measured pose).

**Expected gain** (open-loop replay of 121 episodes at 10 Hz of the same evaluations, 9 plain, 96 RTC and 16 VLASH, through the same controller and arm model, with the rule called the way the client calls it): the median duration ratio of plain episodes goes from 0.830 with v1 to **0.806** with v2 (the total duration of the plain episodes from 0.840 to 0.821), and of all 10 Hz groups together from 0.859 to **0.836**. These numbers come from the source's correction of 2026-11-01: the replay had read the gripper command one row late, and the numbers below the correction were left unchanged. The extra gain comes from the curved chunks: v2 without the gripper prefix also gives 0.806 on plain episodes, so the prefix adds about nothing to the plain median (0.3 to 0.8 points over all groups). With today's 0.2 s setpoint filter the 6 cm guard changes nothing (v2 gives the same result without it); it acts only with the 0.1 s filter. About a fifth of the ticks saved on plain episodes, and about half over all groups, come from chunks that barely move (chord under 5 mm, squeezed to 2 ticks); whether the real policy ends such a pause sooner when it is queried sooner is not modelled. The source gives no corrected values for the 0.1 s filter, the time saved per episode or the arm's distance from its unhurried path; before the correction they were 0.784 and 0.818, 3.40 → 3.70 s per plain episode, and within 1.9 mm (p95; 7.5 mm at most). The safety limits did not change with the correction: the farthest any target was from the arm, 70.7 mm, is the unhurried replay's own maximum, on an RTC chunk executed as given, and no tick passed the client's 10 cm limit. Source: `fr3_accel_test/variable_speed/rule_v2/README.md` (the correction at its top, which takes precedence, then sections 0, 4 and 5) and `CHECK_NOTES.md` section 2(1) in the same folder.

**The gripper.** v2 sends the gripper command with the arm settled. In the study's calibration of the hurried prefix (after the correction, 50 gripper events follow a hurried prefix, all groups), when the gripper command went out the arm was **4.1 mm** (p90) from the pose before the gripper change and moving at **14.4 mm/s** (p90); without acceleration the same events had 7.3 mm and 34.2 mm/s. That is not how the teleoperated demos closed the gripper: there the arm was mostly still moving. Whether grasps change because of it has to be seen on the robot.

**Limits of the evidence (rules v1 and v2).** The replay is open loop: it does not re-run the policy, so how the policy reacts to a faster arm, whether a grasp after a settled approach changes, and the task outcome are unknown. Camera latency is not modelled. The chunks are reconstructed from measured poses and are smoother than the policy's own, so the curved-chunk result is its best case. The plain group has only 9 episodes, so its median moves easily (7 ticks moved v1's from 0.821 to 0.830): compare the total durations too. v2 is experimental and untested on the robot; the study suggests the on/off comparison of v1 on the robot first, then v2.

## Rule v3: the phase clock

v3 is the third rule of the switch and the first that also works with RTC and VLASH. It is a different mechanism, not a tuning of v1 and v2: instead of compressing each chunk and stopping at its end, one **phase** counted in policy rows (1 row is 0.1 s of demonstration time) advances by at most λ rows per 10 Hz control tick, and every tick the target is the chunk interpolated at that phase. The control rate, the client's own 0.10 m / 0.35 rad check against the measured pose, the binarized gripper and the controller settings stay as they are. What keeps it safe:

- the target is held within 0.08 m / 0.28 rad of the measured pose (a soft limit starts at 0.05 m), and two consecutive targets are at most 0.05 m / 0.12 rad apart; when the arm is blocked the last target is sent again, and three such holds in a row ask the policy again (RTC, VLASH; plain holds it until the loop's own next query). An arm pushed so far that the last target is more than 0.08 m from it is followed: the target moves back toward the arm just far enough to sit inside the limit again (to 95 % of it, at most 0.05 m / 0.12 rad per tick, a limit that holds is left alone) instead of pulling the arm back. This step-back is a few millimetres, not 5 cm; a new chunk that starts at the contact still moves the target back (RTC and VLASH up to about 13 mm in one tick through the blend, plain up to the 0.05 m step limit at a re-query). Three such step-backs without a free advance in between also ask the policy again (RTC, VLASH; plain drops its plan and holds until its own next query);
- every gripper change lands on its exact row and is sent once; λ is 1 from 3 rows before to 3 rows after it; a close waits until the arm is within 8 mm and slow, at most about 0.5 s (0.6 s when a plain re-query falls inside the wait). The wait goes on across new chunks and re-queries while the close stays within 16 mm of where the wait began; a close that a new chunk moves further gets its own wait; after 10 waiting ticks (1.0 s) in all a close goes out once it has waited 2 ticks at its current place, and always by 20 (2.0 s). Afterwards λ stays 1 until the fingers have moved and settled; after an open λ stays 1 until the fingers have opened, counted from the tick the gripper reads open (at least 0.3 s, at most 0.8 s; 0.5 s when there is no aperture reading);
- where a chunk is replaced by the next one (RTC) or continued (VLASH, plain), the jump is blended over 2 to 8 ticks (the offset moves at most 25 mm and 0.05 rad per tick, so a bigger jump takes more ticks, up to 8).

**On the page.** With the switch on, the rule choice opens on v3 (the finished rule is on by default). Under it two selects: **Most rows per tick (λ)**, 2.0, 2.5 or 3.0, and **Controller filter (α)**, 0.005 or 0.01. Empty λ ("Default") sends nothing and the server's number applies: `CHUNK_ACCEL_V3_LAMBDA_DEFAULT` in `levi/marvel0/inference_mode.py`, 3.0 (the strongest setting; see Evidence); the command and the result show it. α must be what `set_clip.py --show` reads back from the controller (0.005 today); the page never changes the controller. The RTC execute horizon, the open-loop horizon and the VLASH overlap are the ones of the mode, with their usual defaults.

**The command.** v3 is its own client (`lab/chunk_accel/run_collect_phase.py`, before the VLASH client, so VLASH with v3 is this client too) and always writes `--phase-mode {plain|rtc|vlash}`, `--phase-lambda-max=<λ>` and `--phase-alpha=<α>`, followed by the mode's own flags (`--open-loop-horizon`, `--rtc --rtc-execute-horizon`, `--vlash-overlap-steps`); there is no `--accel-rule`. The policy server is the one of the mode: acceleration does not change what is served, so an RTC command needs the RTC server (the client refuses a plain one). `tests/marvel0/fixtures/chunk_accel_argv.json` holds the golden command of v1, v2 and v3 in each mode; the v1 and v2 entries were captured before v3 existed and must never change.

**The form.** `chunk_accel_rule` `v3`, with `chunk_accel_lambda` and `chunk_accel_alpha` (numbers; empty means the defaults above). The two numbers belong to v3: with another rule or without the switch they are refused (`chunk_accel_v3_field_needs_v3`), and a value the page does not offer too (`chunk_accel_lambda_invalid`, `chunk_accel_alpha_invalid`; values from 1.0 to 3.0 other than 2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0, which the engine accepts for offline sweeps, are never offered). The note, when none is given, is `marvel0 page: <id> chunk accel v3 mode=<mode> lambda=<λ> alpha=<α> horizon=<n>` (`overlap=<n>` for VLASH). The preview names `control.chunk_accel` as `{rule: "v3", mode, lambda_max, alpha}` (v3 has no ramp speed) and warns with `chunk_accel_v3` and `chunk_accel_v3_alpha`; the hosted run record keeps rule, λ and α, and the next episode of a run starts from them. A model folder name can be at most 62 characters now (it was 66), so that the longest default group, `<model>_vlash_accel3_demo`, fits the 80 characters of a group.

**Hiding it.** To drop one mode from v3, remove it from `CHUNK_ACCEL_V3_MODES` in `inference_mode.py` (`chunk_accel_v3_mode_disabled`); to hide v3 altogether, make `CHUNK_ACCEL_RULES` `("v1", "v2")`. The page repeats these constants in `src/components/marvel0/command-logic.ts` (the site answer does not carry them), and `tests/marvel0/test_chunk_accel_runtime.py` fails when the two copies differ, and when a page option is outside what `integrations/chunk_accel/phase.py` accepts. Turning the switch off or choosing rule v1 undoes everything v3 does on the page.

**Evidence.** The design is `fr3_accel_test/decisions/2026-11-01-phase-clock-design.md` (workspace, outside this repository). Its written profile was too cautious: the replay of the built engine gave a duration ratio against the same mode without acceleration of 0.927 / 0.916 for plain, 0.788 / 0.792 for RTC and 0.815 for VLASH (λ 2.0), a speed-up the user could not see on the robot. The curvature limit read the policy's 2–3 mm row-to-row jitter as tight corners and held λ near 1 on a third to half of all ticks. On the user's direction (a clearly visible speed-up balanced with stability, no over-strict limits) the defaults became the "strong" profile on 2026-11-02: curvature measured over 50 mm of arc instead of 15 mm, lateral acceleration 2.4 m/s² (the validated maximum), gripper window 3 rows before and 3 after (was 5 after), λ rising by 0.8 per tick (was 0.6), a close that waits for an arm within 8 mm (was 10), and λ up to 3.0. On the 129 recorded episodes (17 plain, 96 RTC, 16 VLASH), through a lagging-arm model, at λ 3.0 the median ratio is now **0.762 / 0.757 for plain, 0.651 / 0.656 for RTC and 0.605 for VLASH** (the new chunk continues the instruction path / is anchored on the lagging arm), with no rejected jump, no target more than 0.08 m from the arm, no step over 0.05 m and no z-floor clamp that acceleration caused. At a close the arm is within 9.9 mm of the target in RTC (p90; 17.9 mm without acceleration, the line is 10), 7.7 mm in plain and 7.3 mm in VLASH; these numbers include the review fixes of 2026-11-02 (the close wait counted across new chunks, the short settle after an open, which costs VLASH about 0.01 of ratio). What did not pass: plain misses its 0.75 speed line by about 0.01; 2 of 192 RTC and 2 of 32 VLASH runs reach 0.44 m/s target speed (the line is 0.40; the 0.05 m step limit caps it at 0.5); with the long-latency tail the RTC stop overshoots 3.4 mm more than without acceleration (p90, the line is 3); at a task's final stop the modelled arm passes the last target by up to 11 mm in two VLASH episodes (noise-free check); and with 75 mm jumps between chunks a plain close is released up to 38 mm from its row (p90 16.8 mm; 57.6 mm without acceleration). The numbers, the limits that changed and the alternatives that were rejected are in `fr3_accel_test/variable_speed/phase_eval/results/DECISIONS_20261102_strong.md` and the ledger `fr3_accel_test/LIMITS_LEDGER.md` (section E). Every number is a model's: the arm model is about twice as optimistic as the real arm on path error, the replay is open loop (the policy does not react to a faster arm, RTC guidance and the VLASH future state are approximated), and grasp firmness and success can only be measured on the robot. The acceptance on the robot is a paired median ratio of at most 0.75 (target 0.70) with no `Jump exceeds the step limit` message; if grasps slip, choose λ 2.5 or 2.0.

## Limits

Experimental: it has not run on the real robot. Real-robot use needs a supervised check with a hand on the e-stop; the preview warns (`chunk_accel_experimental` for v1 and v2, `chunk_accel_v3` for v3). Rules v1 and v2 are not combined with RTC and VLASH (both schedule chunks themselves); rule v3 is built for them. Install the clients with [the adapter instructions](../integrations/chunk_accel/README.md): v3 needs `phase.py`, `schedulers.py` and `run_collect_phase.py` there, and the page does not check that they are installed.
