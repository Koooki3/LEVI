# In-chunk acceleration (experimental)

The workstation's client command has one more switch: **In-chunk acceleration**. With it, the command runs the FR3 collector through the isolated entry point `lab/chunk_accel/run_collect_accel.py`; the policy server, the collector's gates, clamps, jump checks, keys and recording are unchanged. It works with **plain inference at the policy's 10 Hz only**; asking for it with RTC or VLASH is refused (`chunk_accel_requires_plain`), at another control rate too (`chunk_accel_requires_10hz`), and on a site that does not binarize the gripper (`chunk_accel_requires_binarized_gripper`). Asking for it without naming a mode means plain inference. While it is on, a choice under it picks the rule: **v1** (the default, below) or **v2** ([Rule v2](#rule-v2)). Runs are filed under `<model>_accel_demo` (v1) or `<model>_accel_v2_demo` (v2) unless a group is given.

## What it does

Every chunk the server returns goes through one rule before the collector executes it. A chunk is hurried only if all of these hold:

- no gripper change anywhere in the chunk, including the actions after the executed part (so the approach to a grasp or a release is never hurried), and the same gripper value as the command the arm was last given (the measured state's, which also catches a close the client threw away or sent late);
- at least 1 s of executed actions since the last gripper change (the gripper is still closing or opening). Only actions the collector accepted count: a plan it threw away because of a jump does not shorten the hold;
- the executed actions stay within 5 mm of the straight segment from the first to the last one (a press-and-retract along it is not straight), and the end effector's orientation changes by at most 0.15 rad from the first one.

A hurried chunk is executed as a short ramp from its first to its last executed action, in `m` ticks instead of `n`:

    m = clip(ceil(max(D / (v dt), angle / (w dt), (0.2 s / dt) ln(D / 5 mm))), 2, n)

`D` is the chord, `v` 0.25 m/s, `w` 0.8 rad/s. The last term is sized so that an arm closing in on a held target with a 0.2 s time constant would be within 5 mm of the last action at the next observation. Today's arm does not get that close: in the replay below it was 8.5 mm away (median, 11.2 mm p90) after a hurried chunk, no further than after an unhurried one (8.9 mm median); only the 0.1 s filter reaches about 5 mm (p90 4.9 mm). Along the ramp the target leads by `tau`, the controller's setpoint low-pass (0.2 s, `--accel-tau`), times the ramp speed (the inverse of that low-pass) but never leaves the segment between the first and the last action, and two consecutive targets are at most 5 cm apart (the collector refuses a target 10 cm from the measured pose). Because the hurried chunk is shorter, the collector executes it and queries the policy sooner. After a ramp, at most 3 leading actions of the next chunk that lie behind the ramp's end along its direction are dropped, but only if that chunk keeps moving the same way, so a policy that does not see velocity does not pull the target back.

Each demo records the settings in `policy.chunk_accel` (the session's eval log header too) and the episode's counts in `eval.chunk_accel`, aborted episodes included (LEVI's unattended mode writes them to `eval.abort_detail.chunk_accel`): chunks, hurried, ticks saved, dropped actions, refused jumps, and why the others were not hurried. A chunk with a NaN or infinite pose is passed on exactly as the policy gave it (`not_finite`). The client refuses to start with RTC, VLASH, a control rate other than 10 Hz, step-by-step mode or an unbinarized gripper. Install it (below) before the first run: the page does not check that the client file exists.

## Expected gain

From the latest evaluations of `pi05_fr3_all_step49999` (10 Hz, read-only analysis in the workspace): 48 % of the executed chunks of plain runs, 54 % of RTC runs and 34 % of VLASH runs are straight and gripper-free; 16–17 % contain a gripper change. With this rule (the gripper hold and the settle term included) 46 % of plain chunks are hurried, and an open-loop replay of those episodes through the identified controller and arm model gives a **median 17.0 % shorter plain episode** (duration ratio 0.830; 0.840 for the total duration of the plain episodes) and 0.859 over all 10 Hz groups, RTC and VLASH paths included; these are the numbers of the study's 2026-11-01 correction (see [Rule v2](#rule-v2) for the source), and before it the median plain episode saved 3.40 s. The farthest any target was from the arm, 70.7 mm, is the unhurried replay's own maximum, on an RTC chunk executed as given (the client refuses 10 cm). In the earlier replay of 2026-10-31 the arm's path stayed within 1.6–2.0 mm (p95) of the unhurried one, and the arm was as close to its target at the next observation as without acceleration (median 7–10 mm). The replay does not re-run the policy, so how the policy reacts, and the task outcome, need the real robot. With the setpoint filter at 0.1 s (`setpoint_filter_alpha` 0.01, approved and compiled 2026-10-31, default still 0.005) pass `--accel-tau=0.1` (the page and a hosted run always use 0.2: add the flag to the copied command): it shortens the lead only; the settle term keeps 0.2 s, because the lightly damped arm still rings (with 0.1 s there too it was 24 mm from the target at the next observation, p90, and peaked at 0.46–0.50 m/s).

## Rule v2

v2 is the second rule of the same switch, for comparing the two on the robot. The page shows the choice (two radio buttons, each with a one-line explanation) only while the switch is on; turning the switch off, or choosing RTC, VLASH or another rate, keeps the choice but sends nothing. With v2 the command gets one more flag, `--accel-rule=v2`; a v1 command has none and is exactly the command the switch made before. The form field is `chunk_accel_rule` (`v1` or `v2`, absent means `v1`); v2 without the switch is refused (`chunk_accel_rule_needs_switch`), any other value too: the hosted start answers `chunk_accel_rule_invalid`, the preview route a plain 422 that names the field (its request body is typed). The preview names the rule (`control.chunk_accel.rule`) and adds the warning `chunk_accel_rule_v2`; the hosted run record keeps `chunk_accel_rule` (`v1` for a record made before the choice existed), and each demo records it in `policy.chunk_accel.rule`.

**v2 needs this version of the client.** A client installed before the choice existed has no `--accel-rule`: with `--accel-rule=v2` it stops at start on the unrecognized option (the arm does not move), and a v1 command runs on whatever rule that install has, so neither side of an A/B would be this code. The page does not check the installed version. To update, delete `lab/chunk_accel/accel.py` and `lab/chunk_accel/run_collect_accel.py` in the openpi checkout and run `install.py --apply` again ([the adapter instructions](../integrations/chunk_accel/README.md)); the installer refuses to overwrite files that differ.

What v2 adds to v1 (`integrations/chunk_accel/accel.py`; it is the rule "V4g" of the offline study `fr3_accel_test/variable_speed/rule_v2`, in the workspace, outside this repository):

- **Curved chunks.** A chunk that fails the 5 mm straightness test is re-timed along its own path, as long as the end effector's orientation changes by at most 0.15 rad (v1's rotation limit). How sharply the path itself turns is not limited; the arm only slows down at the bends. The line through its executed actions keeps its shape and gets a speed profile limited by 0.25 m/s, by how much the controller's setpoint filter would cut a corner (at most 2 mm, which allows 0.1 m/s² sideways with today's 0.2 s filter), by 0.8 rad/s and by 1.5 m/s² along the path, ending at rest on the last action. The target leads along the path, never off it. Straight chunks keep v1's ramp.
- **The actions before a gripper change.** The executed actions before the first gripper change (at least 2, at least 1 s after the previous change, passing the same tests) are hurried the same way. The hurried part ends on the action before the gripper change and holds it 2 more ticks (0.2 s), so the gripper command goes out with the arm settled; from the gripper action on, the policy's own pace. A change only after the executed part makes the whole executed part such a prefix.
- **A 6 cm guard.** A hurried plan gets more ticks until no target is predicted more than 6 cm from the arm (a 0.2 s first-order approach from the measured pose).

**Expected gain** (open-loop replay of 121 episodes at 10 Hz of the same evaluations, 9 plain, 96 RTC and 16 VLASH, through the same controller and arm model, with the rule called the way the client calls it): the median duration ratio of plain episodes goes from 0.830 with v1 to **0.806** with v2 (the total duration of the plain episodes from 0.840 to 0.821), and of all 10 Hz groups together from 0.859 to **0.836**. These numbers come from the source's correction of 2026-11-01: the replay had read the gripper command one row late, and the numbers below the correction were left unchanged. The extra gain comes from the curved chunks: v2 without the gripper prefix also gives 0.806 on plain episodes, so the prefix adds about nothing to the plain median (0.3 to 0.8 points over all groups). With today's 0.2 s setpoint filter the 6 cm guard changes nothing (v2 gives the same result without it); it acts only with the 0.1 s filter. About a fifth of the ticks saved on plain episodes, and about half over all groups, come from chunks that barely move (chord under 5 mm, squeezed to 2 ticks); whether the real policy ends such a pause sooner when it is queried sooner is not modelled. The source gives no corrected values for the 0.1 s filter, the time saved per episode or the arm's distance from its unhurried path; before the correction they were 0.784 and 0.818, 3.40 → 3.70 s per plain episode, and within 1.9 mm (p95; 7.5 mm at most). The safety limits did not change with the correction: the farthest any target was from the arm, 70.7 mm, is the unhurried replay's own maximum, on an RTC chunk executed as given, and no tick passed the client's 10 cm limit. Source: `fr3_accel_test/variable_speed/rule_v2/README.md` (the correction at its top, which takes precedence, then sections 0, 4 and 5) and `CHECK_NOTES.md` section 2(1) in the same folder.

**The gripper.** v2 sends the gripper command with the arm settled. In the study's calibration of the hurried prefix (after the correction, 50 gripper events follow a hurried prefix, all groups), when the gripper command went out the arm was **4.1 mm** (p90) from the pose before the gripper change and moving at **14.4 mm/s** (p90); without acceleration the same events had 7.3 mm and 34.2 mm/s. That is not how the teleoperated demos closed the gripper: there the arm was mostly still moving. Whether grasps change because of it has to be seen on the robot.

**Limits of the evidence.** The replay is open loop: it does not re-run the policy, so how the policy reacts to a faster arm, whether a grasp after a settled approach changes, and the task outcome are unknown. Camera latency is not modelled. The chunks are reconstructed from measured poses and are smoother than the policy's own, so the curved-chunk result is its best case. The plain group has only 9 episodes, so its median moves easily (7 ticks moved v1's from 0.821 to 0.830): compare the total durations too. v2 is experimental and untested on the robot; the study suggests the on/off comparison of v1 on the robot first, then v2.

## Limits

Experimental: it has not run on the real robot. Real-robot use needs a supervised check with a hand on the e-stop; the preview warns (`chunk_accel_experimental`). RTC and VLASH are not combined with it (both schedule chunks themselves). Install the client with [the adapter instructions](../integrations/chunk_accel/README.md).
